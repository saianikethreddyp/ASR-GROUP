# ASR Group — Remaining Page Structure

Status: Structure direction v1 for review  
Scope: About Us, Clients, Projects, and Gallery  
Deferred: Contact Us interaction and final copy  
Implementation: Not started; page directions require approval first

## 1. The shared visitor outcome

The pages should collectively answer one decision:

> Is ASR the right, credible, and capable partner for my project—and should I start a conversation?

Each page contributes different evidence:

| Page | Visitor's real question | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| About Us | Who is behind ASR, and can I trust how they work? | People, experience, approach, accountability |
| Clients | Have credible people and organizations trusted ASR? | Approved names, sectors, institutional relationships |
| Projects | Has ASR handled a brief similar to mine? | Complete project stories, scope, location, delivery proof |
| Gallery | Does the finished work visually meet my standard? | Authentic photography, details, materials, range |

Clients, Projects, and Gallery should remain distinct but connected:

- Clients establishes third-party trust.
- Projects helps visitors find work relevant to their brief.
- Gallery holds the complete, project-organized image collections.

Selecting a project opens its matching Gallery folder. A separate long-form project-detail page is not required in the approved direction.

## 2. About Us

### Page outcome

Move the visitor from general brand interest to confidence in the people, operating model, and standards behind the work.

### Recommended section order — reduced to five

1. **Hero — the people and experience behind ASR**
   - Use a strong founders/leadership group portrait or an assured architectural image.
   - One short statement about experience and accountability.
   - Supporting line: 25 years in interiors and 20 years in construction.

2. **Founders and leadership — the central section**
   - This is the most important About Us evidence.
   - Use consistent, professionally photographed headshots.
   - Each profile includes name, verified role, a concise biography, and the responsibility that person carries.
   - Managing Director A. S. Ram Raju can be included now; founder names and all remaining roles require confirmation.
   - Do not mix founders, directors, advisers, and vendors under one unclear “team” label.

3. **Our story and experience**
   - Combine the company story with verified experience figures.
   - Explain why Interiors and Construction belong together.
   - Figures can include 25 years in Interiors, 20 years in Construction, 6 lakh+ sq. ft. constructed, and the carefully worded residential experience claim.

4. **How ASR works**
   - Understand the brief.
   - Coordinate the disciplines.
   - Execute and hand over.
   - Supporting capabilities are mentioned within this flow rather than becoming a separate card grid.

5. **Closing invitation**
   - “Planning something significant?”
   - One primary action: Discuss Your Project.

Institutional proof belongs primarily on Clients and Projects. Branch information belongs on Contact and in the footer. This keeps About Us focused on people, story, and working method.

### Evidence required before final design/copy

- Approved founder and leadership headshots.
- Confirmed names, titles, founder/director status, responsibilities, and biographies.
- Founding year and reliable company milestones.
- Exact legal company name for public use.
- Confirmed figures and approved wording.
- Current branch presence and addresses.
- Permission to name professional advisers.

### What to avoid

- A generic vision/mission/values card grid near the top.
- A long corporate timeline without meaningful milestones.
- Repeating the homepage capability section.
- Claims such as “industry-leading” or “best-in-class” without proof.

## 3. Clients

### Page outcome

Help a visitor recognize that organizations and project types with serious requirements have trusted ASR.

### Recommended section order

1. **Hero — trust across different briefs**
   - Short, assured introduction.
   - Visual emphasis on approved client relationships rather than a decorative hero.

2. **Selected relationships**
   - A restrained field of 8–12 priority names or logos.
   - Prioritize recognizable and strategically relevant relationships.
   - Avoid presenting every historical name at equal weight.

3. **Experience across sectors**
   - Technology and business.
   - Industry and enterprise.
   - Developers and project organizations.
   - Institutions and public-sector environments.
   - Hospitality, retail, and branded environments.
   - Use concise editorial groupings, not separate card stacks.

4. **Selected client engagements**
   - Two or three evidence-rich engagements.
   - Each item should show client/project, ASR scope, location, sector, and a link to the project story.
   - Use only engagements whose scope and imagery are approved.

5. **Residential experience**
   - Explain the approximately 4,000–5,000 flats and villas claim carefully.
   - List selected communities only when ASR’s contribution can be described accurately.
   - Do not imply ASR developed or fully delivered a community when its role was limited.

6. **Institutional confidence**
   - C.M. Camp Office.
   - Telangana Legislative Assembly.
   - Jubilee Hall restoration.

7. **Closing invitation**
   - One action: Start a Project Conversation.

### Evidence required

- Permission to display every client name and logo.
- Exact ASR scope for every featured relationship.
- Confirmation of whether work was direct, subcontracted, advisory, or execution-only.
- Confirmation of the United Nations and SGS references.
- Accurate residential contribution wording.

## 4. Projects

### Page outcome

Allow a visitor to quickly find credible work similar to their own brief, then understand what ASR actually delivered.

### Projects listing structure

1. **Hero**
   - Lead with the strongest verified project image.
   - One concise statement about carrying intent through execution.

2. **Featured project**
   - One large, current, evidence-rich project story.
   - Use BMW only after resolving the conflicting location and scope information.

3. **Primary filters**
   - All Projects.
   - Interiors.
   - Construction.

4. **Contextual filters**
   - Show only after Interiors is selected:
     - Residential.
     - Corporate & Commercial.
     - Branded Environments.
   - Do not show every filter at once.

5. **Project index**
   - Large editorial images rather than a dense card grid.
   - Every preview includes:
     - Project name.
     - Discipline and sector.
     - Location.
     - ASR scope in one line.
     - Status: completed, ongoing, or illustrative/render.
   - Selecting a preview opens the corresponding Gallery folder.

6. **Closing invitation**
   - “Have a similar brief—or something entirely different?”
   - One action: Discuss Your Project.

### Projects-to-Gallery behavior

- Recommended URL: `/gallery/[project-slug]`.
- This is a Gallery collection route, not a conventional project-detail article.
- It opens with the selected project name, location, category, verified ASR scope, and complete image collection.
- “Back to all projects” returns to the Projects index.
- “View all gallery collections” returns to the unfiltered Gallery.
- This preserves a shareable URL for each project while keeping the experience visual and simple.

### Project data required

- Project title and approved client name.
- Discipline, sector, location, year, and status.
- Exact ASR scope.
- Genuine images and ownership/usage permission.
- Approved captions and photographer attribution.
- Measurable project facts where available.
- Client quotation or outcome evidence where available.

### Accuracy hold

BMW locations conflict across current sources: one source states Faridabad; another draft states Jubilee Hills and Mangalagiri. Do not publish a location until the client resolves this.

Generated or illustrative images must retain a clear disclosure and must never be presented as original project photography.

## 5. Gallery

### Page outcome

Give visitors a fast, high-quality visual answer to: “Does this work meet my standard?”

### Recommended section order

1. **Compact gallery introduction**
   - Keep the opening short so authentic work appears within the first viewport.
   - The first image should be one of the strongest verified photographs in the entire portfolio.

2. **Progressive filters**
   - All.
   - Residential Interiors.
   - Corporate & Commercial.
   - Branded Environments.
   - Construction.
   - On mobile, use a clear horizontally scrollable filter row or a familiar category control; do not compress five labels into tiny buttons.

3. **Editorial image field**
   - Use a measured masonry or justified layout with varied but controlled image sizes.
   - Lead with full-space images, then alternate with details and material moments.
   - Do not add text overlays to every image.

4. **Image viewer**
   - Previous and next controls.
   - Close control.
   - Project, category, location, and short caption.
   - “View project details” only when a corresponding project page exists.
   - Keyboard, touch, and swipe behavior should be explicit and accessible.

5. **Closing invitation**
   - Let a visitor reference an image or project when starting an enquiry.

### Mobile behavior

- Use one strong image per row for primary photographs.
- Two-column crops may be used sparingly for details, never for important wide compositions.
- No image reveal may depend on hover.
- Scroll animation must not hide core evidence when JavaScript, observers, or reduced motion behave differently.
- Preserve image aspect ratios and avoid aggressive crops of rooms or building elevations.

### Gallery metadata required

- Original file.
- Project name.
- Category and discipline.
- Location.
- Image caption or depicted zone.
- Orientation and preferred crop.
- Photographer/owner.
- Usage permission.
- Original, render, illustrative, or process-image status.

### What to avoid

- Using the Gallery as a second Projects page.
- Mixing generated imagery with genuine photography without a clear label.
- A dense wall of visually similar thumbnails.
- Opening every image without useful context.

## 6. Cross-page behavior

- A client engagement can link to its Gallery collection when images exist.
- A Project opens its full Gallery collection.
- A Gallery collection can link back to the Projects index.
- Enquiries from a Project or Gallery should preserve the originating project/image as context.
- The header and footer should use real page routes once page implementation begins; homepage anchors should not remain the final navigation model.

## 7. Psychology hypotheses and checks

| Observed signal | Possible mechanism | User risk | Recommended change | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current drafts contain long capability and client lists | Cognitive load and choice overload | Visitors scan without finding personally relevant proof | Progressive categories and a small number of featured examples | Ask target users to find relevant evidence in under 20 seconds |
| Projects, Clients, and Gallery overlap in subject matter | Goal-oriented behavior | Visitors cannot predict which page will answer their question | Give each page one distinct evidence role | Navigation comprehension test with five target users |
| Premium imagery is a major decision input | Affective first response | Weak or late imagery reduces confidence before credentials are read | Place the strongest authentic image near the top | Compare project-detail visits and qualified enquiries |
| Internal scope is currently unclear for some named work | Curse of knowledge | Visitors may overestimate or misunderstand ASR's responsibility | State ASR scope in plain language on every project | Test whether users can correctly describe ASR's role |
| Important content uses reveal animation | Selective attention plus a technical failure risk | Evidence can remain hidden, especially on mobile | Core images remain visible even if motion does not complete | Test reduced motion, slow devices, direct hash links, and mobile widths |

These are design hypotheses, not claims about every visitor. Stronger evidence would come from short comprehension tests with premium homeowners, facility decision-makers, and architect/consultant partners.

## 8. Typography research direction

### Recommended direction

**Newsreader for display headings + Instrument Sans for body, navigation, labels, and project facts.**

- Newsreader introduces a warm editorial voice suited to architectural storytelling and long-form project pages.
- Instrument Sans has the precision of a contemporary grotesk but enough character to avoid looking like the current Manrope-led reference.
- The contrast gives ASR two useful voices: expressive and human for major statements; controlled and technical for facts and navigation.

Suggested use:

- Display headings: Newsreader, regular, restrained optical sizing.
- Body: Instrument Sans, regular.
- Navigation and labels: Instrument Sans, medium.
- Large facts: Instrument Sans, regular or medium.
- Maximum of two font families.

### Lower-risk alternative

**Newsreader for display headings + retain Manrope temporarily for body/UI.**

This creates an immediate distinction with minimal implementation change, but the inherited Manrope character will still be visible in navigation, buttons, and body copy.

### Sans-only alternative

**Instrument Sans throughout.**

This is the cleanest operational system and still differentiates ASR from Manrope, but it sacrifices some of the warmth and editorial contrast that suits the current imagery and ivory/charcoal palette.

### Recommendation before a site-wide change

Create one controlled typography proof using the existing homepage hero, one project title, a body paragraph, navigation, and project facts. Review it at desktop and 390 px mobile before replacing the global font.
